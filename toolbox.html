Tool 1 · The word-problem translator

English and maths are two languages. This is the dictionary. When a word problem confuses you, find the phrase below and translate it.

Words you seeMaths you writeExample
sum, total, altogether, combined+ (add)"total of 84 marbles" → r + b = 84
difference, how many more, left, remaining− (subtract)"12 more red than blue" → r = b + 12
each, per, every, groups of, twice× (multiply)"twice Meera's age" → 2 × m
shared equally, split, ratio parts÷ (divide)"split ₹750 equally" → 750 ÷ 3
is, was, will be, results in= (equals)"the total is 37" → … = 37
more than+ after the thing"4 more than m" → m + 4 not 4 + m first!
less thansubtract from it"5 less than x" → x − 5
times as old/heavy as×"7 times as old as Vikram" → 7 × v
per cent of÷ 100 ×"24% of 250" → 250 × 24 ÷ 100
Worked translation: "Rohan is 4 years older than twice Meera's age. Rohan is 16."
↓
"twice Meera's age" → 2 × m   "4 years older than that" → 2 × m + 4   "Rohan is 16" → 2m + 4 = 16 ✔
The golden move: always give the unknown a letter first (x, m, p — anything), then translate the story sentence by sentence into that letter. The equation writes itself.

Why digit sums work for 3 and 9: 10 is 1 more than 9, so every group of ten "leaks" exactly 1 into the digit sum. That is why only the digits matter for 3 and 9 — and why the trick fails for 8, where 10 does not leak a multiple of 8. Rules you understand beat rules you memorise.

Tool 3 · Prime numbers — the atoms of maths

What is a prime? A number with exactly two factors: 1 and itself. 7 = 1 × 7 only → prime. 9 = 3 × 3 → not prime.

Careful: 1 is NOT prime (it has only one factor), and 2 is the only even prime.

The primes up to 50 — know these by heart

23579 1113151719 2123252729 3133353739 4143454749

(Green = prime. Notice: after 2, every prime is odd — but most odd numbers are not prime!)

The square-root test — is 91 prime?
↓
√91 ≈ 9.5 → only test primes up to 9: that is 2, 3, 5, 7.
91 is odd, digit sum 10 (not ÷3), doesn't end in 0/5…
but 91 = 7 × 13. Not prime!
If a number has any factor, one factor must be ≤ its square root — so testing up to √n is enough. That saves half the work.
Famous trap numbers — the "looks prime but isn't" gang
51 = 3 × 17  ·  57 = 3 × 19
87 = 3 × 29  ·  91 = 7 × 13
119 = 7 × 17  ·  143 = 11 × 13
187 = 11 × 17  ·  209 = 11 × 19
None end in 0/2/5 and none have digit sums divisible by 3 — they LOOK prime. Test 7 and 11 before believing them.

Tool 4 · Lightning calculation tricks

×11 magic

34 × 11

Split 3 _ 4 and drop the digit-sum 3+4=7 in the middle: 374.
Carry if the sum exceeds 9: 87 × 11 → 8_(15)_7 → 8+1=9, 5 → 957.

×5, ×50

86 × 5

×10 then halve: 860 ÷ 2 = 430.
For ×50: ×100 then halve: 86 × 50 = 8600 ÷ 2 = 4300.

×25

28 × 25

25 is a quarter of 100: ×100 then ÷4 → 2800 ÷ 4 = 700.
Handy ÷4 trick: halve twice (2800 → 1400 → 700).

×9, ×99

47 × 99

One less than ×100: 4700 − 47 = 4653.
Same idea for 9: 47 × 9 = 470 − 47 = 423.

squares ending in 5

75 × 75

Take 7 × (7+1) = 56, stick 25 after it: 5625.
Works for every number ending in 5: 45² = 2025, 95² = 9025.

difference of squares

48 × 52

Numbers equally spaced around 50: 50² − 2² = 2500 − 4 = 2496.
Pattern: (a−b)(a+b) = a² − b². Magic for near-round products.

Tool 5 · The champion's four steps (never skip #4)

1 · Understand

List every fact. Circle the numbers. Underline the question. If you can't say what's given, you're not ready for step 2.

2 · Plan

Name the unknown x. Translate sentences into maths using the dictionary above. One equation = one lock.

3 · Solve

Undo operations in reverse order. Small, careful moves beat one big leap. Write every step — olympiad marks live in steps.

4 · Check

Put your answer back into the story. Is it sensible? Can Meera be 60 years old when Rohan is 16? No — fix it.
